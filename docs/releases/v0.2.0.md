# Stabileo 0.2.0: the safety release

**English** · [Español](v0.2.0.es.md) · [Português](v0.2.0.pt.md)

Released 2026-10-18. The full technical record is in [`CHANGELOG.md`](../../CHANGELOG.md); the
merge record of the safety wave, with every conflict and how it was resolved, is in
[`docs/MERGE_PLAN_SAFETY_WAVE.md`](../MERGE_PLAN_SAFETY_WAVE.md).

> **Note on timing.** stabileo.com deploys every change merged into `main`, so everything below
> has been live since 17–18 October 2026, before these notes existed. If you opened a project
> after that date and got different numbers than before, this is why. We are sorry for not saying
> so at the time; from this release on, releases that change results are announced in the app.

This release is the outcome of a verification campaign. We checked the solver against
independent programs, against symbolic derivations, against published worked examples and
against the code texts, clause by clause. We found errors, and many of them were on the
**unsafe** side: capacities too high, loads too low, results that ignored part of the model.
They are fixed. Where the program cannot compute something safely with the data it has, it now
says "not evaluated" or refuses the model by name instead of returning a number.

---

## 1. Your results may change: re-run your projects

**The same model can give different numbers in 0.2.0.** Open each project you are working on,
solve it again and review its design checks before you use earlier results. Most changes lower
capacities or raise demands. A few raise a result (for example, a buckling factor that was
wrongly low). Some models that used to solve are now refused, with a message saying why.

Below is every change that affects results, grouped by who is affected.

### 1.1 Defaults and inputs

| Change | Who is affected |
|---|---|
| **Default dead-load build-up: 2.32 → 2.52 kN/m².** The partition allowance is now the CIRSOC 101-2025 §4.3.2 minimum of 0.75 kN/m² of floor. A partition allowance below 0.75 kN/m² is reported as missing (floors with a live load above 4 kN/m² are exempt, §3.1.4). | Projects that use the default floor build-up or a smaller partition allowance. Gravity loads go up about 0.2 kN/m². |
| **Lateral-torsional buckling now assumes the load acts on the top flange** unless you say otherwise. CIRSOC 301 (F.2.4b–F.2.6b), EC3 (Mcr with the load height zg = +h/2, NCCI SN003b eq. 2) and AISC 360 (Cb divided by 1.4). EC3 refuses a top-flange load when the section depth is not known. | Every steel beam checked for LTB. Flexural capacities of unbraced beams go down. If your load really acts at the shear centre or the bottom flange, say so in the member's data. |
| **CIRSOC 301 tension rupture needs the end connection.** Rupture now uses Ae = An·U (holes and shear lag). Before, it took Ae = Ag, which is optimistic for every bolted member and for members connected through only some of their elements. Without an end connection the member's tension check is **not evaluated** (shown as such, never as a pass). Ae = Ag is never assumed. | Every steel member in tension. Add the end connection in Specifications › Members (see 3.1) to get the rupture check. |
| **Weld groups and fillet welds are treated as end-loaded by default** (β = 1.2 − 0.002·L/w, and an effective length of 180w beyond 300w). Before, a long fillet was credited in full, or with 0.6·L beyond 300w. You can opt out for welds that are not end-loaded. | Long fillet welds in joints and weld groups. Capacities go down. |
| **Sections are taken as welded** unless they were picked from a rolled catalogue family. This uses the more conservative residual stress, flange classification and buckling curves. | Parametric, drawn or imported steel sections. |
| **Material presets corrected.** IRAM F-24/F-26/F-36 steels: E = 200 000 MPa, G = 77 200 MPa (was 210 000 / 80 769, from the 1984 code; models were 5 % too stiff). Reinforced-concrete unit weight 25 kN/m³ (was 24–24.5). NBR concrete uses the secant modulus. Aluminium 5052-H32 and 6063-T6 from EN 1999. | Models in those materials: deflections, drifts and self-weight change. |
| **A utilisation above 1.0 fails.** Before, CIRSOC 301 showed "warning" up to 1.05 and another path up to 1.10. | Members with ratios between 1.00 and 1.10. |

### 1.2 Loads and combinations (CIRSOC 101 / 102 / 103 / 104)

All of these were on the unsafe side.

- **Combinations with absent companion loads are now generated** (CIRSOC 101-2025 §2.3.2).
  Before, roof suction under wind lowered every gravity combination. In the `pro-simple-shed`
  example the column axial force went from 44 to 62 kN.
- **Seismic combination: f2 = 0.70·S on roofs that keep snow** (INPRES-CIRSOC 103 Tabla 3.3),
  not 0.2. The larger factor of the two codes is used for L and S. The seismic weight now
  includes f2·S.
- **Wind on pitched roofs:** CIRSOC 102 roof Cp now interpolates in h/L and follows note 2 past
  the 0.0 entry. This affects h/L other than 0.25, 0.5 or 1.0 and slopes between 35° and 60°.
- **Live-load reduction:** each member uses its own tributary area (before, every member took
  the largest area in the model). One-way slabs are not reduced unless the load is heavy
  (§4.7.6).
- Static seismic method refused for group Ao with medium irregularity (the modal method is
  required). Snow table additions (Uspallata, Villa Reynolds).

### 1.3 Concrete design checks (CIRSOC 201, ACI 318): lower capacities

- **CIRSOC 201 flexure by strain compatibility** for tension and compression steel. Before, φMn
  could be up to 18 % too high. A compression bar is not credited while the neutral axis is above
  it, and the concrete it displaces is deducted.
- **CIRSOC 201 steel yield and φ:** the compression-controlled limit is εty = fy/Es for every
  grade (it was a fixed 0.002). The tension-controlled limit is 0.005 (2005 edition) or
  εty + 0.003 (2025 edition).
- **The default CIRSOC 201 edition is now 2025.**
- **CIRSOC 201 shear:** Vc = (1/6)·√f'c·bw·d per the 2005 text (it used ACI's 0.17), √f'c capped
  at 8.3 MPa, and stirrup fyt capped at 420 MPa.
- **New CIRSOC 201 ductility check (§9.3.3.1).**
- **Zero reinforcement or zero capacity is "not evaluated" and fails.** Before, a member with no
  flexural capacity and no moment could pass.
- **Crack width** was 6.9 times too wide (that error was on the conservative side).
- **ACI 318:** flexure by strain compatibility (Mn was 14.5 % high in one case). A **new axial
  check** (`axialRatio`): an axially overloaded column used to pass. Flexure is flagged when Pu is
  outside the interaction diagram, and a T-beam with axial force is flagged incomplete.
- Footing shear: λs added, and row (a) is not used without stirrups.

### 1.4 Steel and cold-formed design checks: lower capacities or refusals

- **CIRSOC 301:** web shear per §G.2.1 (one published example was 60 % unconservative).
  Slender-element columns (§E.7, Q factor). Noncompact and slender girders (§F.3–F.6). LTB in
  the code's own form (it used AISC 360-10's). Torsional and flexural-torsional buckling (§E.4)
  for I, tee and channel columns: a tee at 3 m dropped from 451.8 to 392.8 kN. Without J or Cw
  where they are needed, the check is refused. **Members bent about both axes without axial
  force are now checked by H.1.1b**, which was skipped before.
- **CIRSOC 301 returns "not evaluated"** for shapes and cases it cannot classify (cold-formed
  shapes, strong-axis flexure of non-I shapes, angles in flexure), never a pass.
- **EC3:** member interaction per 6.3.3 with Annex B factors (it was a linear sum with k = 1).
  Class 4 effective properties are computed for I and RHS sections, and refused without them.
  χLT = 1 for Lb = 0 (it was NaN). A Cm outside [0.4, 1] is refused.
- **AISC 360:** flange local buckling F3/F6.2. Slender-element columns (E7): refused without
  the element data (`compressionElements`).
- **AISI S100 (cold-formed):** the F2.1 formula was corrected, G2.1 shear (up to 51 % high
  before), and the interaction is the linear sum. Zero capacity is "not evaluated".
- **Connections:** bolt bearing uses Lc = s − dh for interior bolts (CIRSOC 301 §J.3.10; it
  overstated bearing). Combined tension and shear no longer divides by φ twice (this was
  conservative).
- **Section properties:** the shear centre and warping constant of asymmetric sections such as
  angles, Z and lipped sections now account for the product of inertia (an L100×10 had Cw 24
  times too large). Thin-walled J was 18–24 % low and is now within about 3 % (still a lower
  bound). A clockwise-drawn asymmetric section had mirrored principal axes.

### 1.5 Analysis results

- **Inclined supports in every analysis.** Modal, harmonic, spectral, Craig–Bampton, buckling,
  time history, SSI, fiber, arc length and contact now work in the support's own frame, like
  the linear solver did. A rotated portal frame gave 6.31 Hz instead of 5.67 Hz, and a column
  guided by an inclined support buckled at 11.4 instead of 30.9. Displacement control still
  refuses inclined supports.
- **Constraints on supported nodes are now enforced.** If a rigid link, diaphragm, equal-DOF or
  MPC slave sits on a support, the support used to win and the constraint was dropped, with
  only an `OverConstrainedDof` warning. Constraints at inclined-support nodes are now built
  correctly in every analysis. Contradictory sets, such as two rigid links on one slave, are
  refused.
- **Several supports on one node:** plain restraints merge. Springs, settlements or inclined
  data on a shared node are refused (they used to double-count the reaction or silently pick
  one).
- **Buckling:** the axial force is read from each member's elongation. With axial point or
  partial loads, λ changes (by 3× in one case). 2D geometric stiffness is condensed at hinges:
  a frame with a pin-ended member used to buckle at λ ≈ 5e-6. Curved-shell buckling was 200×
  too high. (An interaction between two fixes that would have halved every shell buckling
  factor was caught and fixed before release; it never reached the app.)
- **P-Delta:** member end forces now include the geometric stiffness (K + Kg)·u. Before, they
  balanced neither the joints nor the reactions. 3D P-Delta includes shell geometric stiffness.
- **Ground-motion time history** includes the mass coupled to the supports. Up to about 50 % of
  the earthquake load was dropped.
- **Modal:** models with massless DOFs (hinged members, truss-only nodes) solve instead of
  failing.
- **Plastic collapse:** 2D overestimated collapse and ignored unloading hinges. 3D formed hinges
  only at member ends (up to 2.3× unsafe). 2D now forms hinges at rotational springs,
  constraints and connectors (one case went from 150 to 83.3). 3D plastic refuses constraints,
  connectors and springs.
- **Connectors:** a finite-length shear spring now carries its moment V·L, and connectors in
  all-truss models are assembled on the right DOFs. Connectors are now supported in the
  nonlinear solvers.
- **Co-rotational 3D and contact:** exact large rotations, and member forces include the member
  loads (they were qL²/12 short). Contact reports the converged state (2D used to report the
  linear solution), gaps act from closure, and Coulomb sliding is modelled. An unconverged
  solve reports how much of the load reached equilibrium.
- **Shells and solid shells:** solid shells are frame-invariant (Scordelis-Lo 0.39 → 0.95 of the
  reference), and warped MITC4 quads are corrected. Solid shells meshed together with frames or
  shells now solve.
- **Timoshenko members** use their own fixed-end forces (13.6 % off on a short cantilever before).
  Spectral member forces no longer include static member loads. HHT-α is now true HHT-α.
- **Singular-stiffness threshold** tightened from 64ε to 1e-10. Mechanisms that used to solve
  silently, such as a dangling hinged member, are flagged. There is a new 3D warning,
  `stiffness_not_positive_definite`.

### 1.6 New refusals instead of silent results

When an analysis cannot handle a feature of your model, it now **refuses it and names the
feature and where it is**, instead of solving a simplified model without a word. Examples:
prescribed displacements in co-rotational, arc length, fiber, SSI or harmonic analyses; thermal
loads in co-rotational and arc length; connectors in staged construction; a nodal moment on a
node with no rotational stiffness; a 3D inclined support without a usable normal. The complete
list, cell by cell, is [`docs/verification/FEATURE_MATRIX.md`](../verification/FEATURE_MATRIX.md).

If a project that used to solve is now refused, the message says which feature and which
element. That project's earlier results were computed on a different model from the one you
drew.

### 1.7 New warnings: the model sanity review

The PRO **Diagnostics** panel now reviews the model for modelling errors the solver cannot see.
It checks equilibrium per case, gravity loads pointing up or sideways, missing self-weight,
weight per floor area, the fundamental period against the code estimate, tributary loads
against axial forces, likely unit mix-ups (E in GPa, millimetre geometry), parts with no path to
a support, and nodes lying on a member without being connected to it. **New warnings on an old
project are worth reading.** Each one says what was found, why it matters and how to fix it.

### 1.8 Example models corrected

The sanity review found real modelling errors in several gallery examples, and they are fixed.
Their results change, in some cases by a lot:

- **3d-building, space-frame, torre-irregular-con-retiros:** gravity loads were given in local
  Y, which is horizontal. Dead vertical reaction: 2 695 → 4 135 kN, 8 481 → 16 161 kN and
  79 132 → 124 910 kN.
- **3d-nave-industrial:** 20 unconnected nodes were split into the members, and the wind case is
  now a consistent "Wind +Y".
- **cable-stayed bridges, offshore-platform, stadium-canopy, suspension-bridge, full-stadium,
  la-bombonera, pipe-rack, xl-diagrid-tower:** duplicated or unconnected members, missing
  bracing, loads in the wrong direction or loads that were never given. The large deflections
  some of them showed are gone.
- Several small 3D examples had lateral loads in the Dead Load case. They are now in Wind.

If you started a project from one of these examples, compare it with the corrected example.

### 1.9 Other

- The feedback form requires a captcha. Share links, Excel imports and AI requests have size and
  rate limits.
- Detailing stamps loaded from a file show as stale until the model is solved again.

## 2. Safer by design

These are the checks that found the errors above. They keep running.

- **Certified results.** For linear static, modal, buckling, P-Delta, spectral, harmonic,
  time-history and plastic analyses, the engine computes a rigorous bound on the numerical error
  of the solution, in outward-rounded interval arithmetic. The bound is proved in Lean
  ([`docs/verification/CERTIFY.md`](../verification/CERTIFY.md)). Diagrams, extremes, deflections and
  reactions show "± bound" in their tooltips. **What a certificate proves:** that the numbers
  solve the equations the program assembled, within the stated bound. **It does not prove** that
  the model or the element formulation is right; the other checks below cover that. Some cases
  carry no bound (for example, reactions at constraints and inclined supports, and the
  co-rotational and material-nonlinear solvers), and the program says so.
- **Design checks re-checked by the engine.** Every CIRSOC 201 and CIRSOC 301 result in the app
  is recomputed by the engine's certified check. A pass the engine does not confirm is shown as
  a failure (`VERIF_ENGINE_DISAGREES`), and a check that could not be confirmed says "not
  verified". Certified interval checks also exist for AISC 360, EC3, EC2, ACI 318 and AISI S100.
  Masonry, connections, foundations and serviceability checks are not certified.
- **Differential testing against 7 independent programs:** OpenSees, Code_Aster, Frame3DD, PyNite,
  MYSTRAN, Kratos and CalculiX. The same random models are solved in each one, and
  displacements, reactions, member forces, frequencies and buckling factors are compared.
- **Symbolic derivation** of the frame element matrices in SymPy, compared exactly with the Rust
  code.
- **Published worked examples** reproduced clause by clause (INTI CIRSOC 201/301, AISC Design
  Examples, SCI/SBE EC3 examples). Each is a regression test.
- **Load tables and combinations** re-read from the official CIRSOC texts.
- **Feature × analysis matrix:** every model feature in every analysis is checked against an
  equivalent model or an analytical value. A cell that silently ignores a feature fails the test
  suite.
- **Cross-path assembly oracle:** the dense, sparse, parallel and constrained assemblers must
  produce the same matrices, entry by entry.
- **Fuzzing** of the WASM entry points and **mutation testing**, plus a security audit of the
  backend, imports and supply chain.
- **The app's TypeScript engineering logic** was checked against the engine with 5 000-section
  property tests, and the CIRSOC 301 checker against the engine on 4 000 random members.

## 3. New features

### 3.1 Tension connections (PRO)
An **End connection (tension)** card in Specifications › Members and in the steel workflow.
Describe each member end as bolted (diameter, hole type, bolts per line, lines, pitch, optional
stagger) or welded (longitudinal, transverse or both, weld length), and say which elements are
connected. The program computes An (B.4.2.2), U (D.3) and Ae, previews them on each member's own
section before you apply, and shows the rupture check with its clause references. Members without
a connection appear in a "not evaluated, add the end connection" list with a button that takes
you to the member. Tubes (CIRSOC 302), cold-formed sections (CIRSOC 303) and other cases outside
the clause are refused by name.

### 3.2 Steel results screen
Every CIRSOC 301 check of every member, with its ratio, the governing check, the status and its
reason, a "proven bound" badge when the engine certified it, and the calculation with clause
references. In English, Spanish and Portuguese.

### 3.3 Angles and double angles in steel checks
Catalogue single angles (equal and unequal) and back-to-back double angles now reach the
CIRSOC 301 check: E.5 for single angles (you state the end condition) and E.6 for double angles
(you state the connectors). Angles in flexure (F.9, F.10) are refused by name.

### 3.4 Model sanity review
See 1.7. It is in the PRO Diagnostics panel.

### 3.5 Smaller additions
- Deflection curves include the Timoshenko shear deflection.
- The status bar shows the app version, and a one-time notice links these notes when a release
  changes results.

## 4. Known limitations

- **Old projects are not re-checked automatically.** This release shows one notice for all
  projects. A per-project prompt ("this project uses features whose results changed") is being
  built.
- **Not everything is certified.** Certificates cover the numerical solution, not the model.
  The co-rotational and material-nonlinear solvers, rotations along deflection curves, and some
  reactions are not bounded. Masonry, connections, foundations and serviceability checks are
  computed in ordinary floating point.
- **Displacement control** still refuses inclined supports.
- **Solid shells:** no enhanced assumed strains, so thin plates show Poisson thickness locking
  (Navier plate 0.84 of the reference). The hemisphere benchmark is still membrane-locked.
- **Co-rotational convergence:** on subdivided models the convergence test can stall just above
  its tolerance. An unconverged solve reports only the load that reached equilibrium.
- **CIRSOC 101/103:** the open-building free-roof minimum (§2.1.5), ice and extraordinary
  combinations are not generated yet.
- **CIRSOC 301:** strong-axis flexure of non-I shapes (§F.7–F.11) and angles in flexure are not
  implemented and are refused. Footing and some load coefficients are still computed in
  TypeScript, outside the certified engine.
- **Verification jobs:** the nightly fuzzing, mutation and differential jobs exist, but a
  failure does not yet alert anyone automatically.
- **Example models:** some remain simplified on purpose and say so (la-bombonera's live load is a
  token load; the suspension bridge is solved linearly on unstressed geometry, so read its
  forces, not its deflections; the cable-stayed bridge's stays have no pretension).
- The full list of open items is in section 5 of
  [`docs/MERGE_PLAN_SAFETY_WAVE.md`](../MERGE_PLAN_SAFETY_WAVE.md).

## Changes deployed earlier

There was no 0.1.0 release announcement. The changes listed in `CHANGELOG.md` before October 2026
were deployed as they were merged. Some of them also changed results: shell sign conventions and
the DKT triangle (September 2026), the reversed direction of shell edge loads (August 2026) and the
CIRSOC 201 provided-reinforcement format (July 2026). If a project predates those changes, the
advice is the same: solve it again.
